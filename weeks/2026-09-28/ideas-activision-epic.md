## Activision: ideas beyond the 9/30 deck

The deck covers conquest (Battlefield), lapsed Call of Duty homes and Switch 2. These add to it.

1. **Pre-order to day-one install ladder.** MW4 has campaign early access from Oct 16. Retarget homes that saw CTV with a QR to the pre-order page, then suppress converters so spend moves to the next group. KPI is pre-orders, not reach.
2. **Warzone reactivation.** Season 1 connects Warzone to MW4 content and progression. Target homes with Warzone or Black Ops 7 installed that have gone quiet in the last 30 to 60 days. They are the cheapest people to bring back.
3. **Game Pass hold-out play.** MW4 is not on Game Pass at launch. Homes with the Xbox or Game Pass app but a PlayStation console signal are a clean "buy it now" audience. Check the data path with Activision legal.
4. **Switch 2 household launch.** First Call of Duty on Nintendo in 13 years. Sizing the Switch 2 app and companion-app base is the first thing to build `[CONFIRM]`.
5. **Creator TV® takeover for the launch week.** Gaming and gaming-adjacent creators on Sabio's own channel with a launch-week integration. Creator fit has to be cleared before any name goes in a deck.
6. **Esports and streaming-viewer audiences.** Homes with Twitch, YouTube Gaming or Call of Duty League app signals, reached on CTV while they watch. Good for the 18 to 34 gap that broadcast misses.
7. **Defensive flight against GTA VI.** From Nov 19 the share of attention drops. Hold a lighter always-on flight into the holidays on lapsed and sports-gaming homes instead of going dark.

Ask for lunch: which of Battlefield conquest, Warzone reactivation or Switch 2 they would test first, and whether the RED team wants a deal ID on Omnicom seats.

## Epic Games: first ideas

Epic has the opposite problem from Activision: huge reach, so the work is *who to bring back and what to promote next*.

1. **Fortnitemares to Chapter 8 bridge.** Season ends Oct 31, mini-season after, Chapter 8 leaked for late Nov or early Dec. Target lapsed Fortnite homes (app installed, low recent use) with a "new chapter" message timed to the real launch date once Epic confirms it.
2. **Mobile return.** Fortnite is back on the App Store in the US and EU and on Google Play worldwide. Homes with a teen or young adult on an iPhone who dropped Fortnite when it left iOS are a natural win-back group. Measure installs.
3. **Epic Games Store mobile.** Epic's own store launched on mobile in August. Sell the store to Android and iPhone homes that already buy games in apps. KPI is store installs and first purchase.
4. **Creator-made islands.** Epic says it has paid more than $1B to UEFN creators and island playtime on mobile more than doubled. Promote a rotating set of creator islands to homes that follow gaming creators. Pairs naturally with Creator TV®.
5. **Unreal Engine for brands and developers.** A second, B2B audience: developers and studios, reached through professional app and content signals on CTV in work-from-home homes. Smaller, but a different budget line.
6. **Gifting and V-Bucks for the holidays.** Parent households with a Fortnite-playing child, reached on CTV for gift cards and the Item Shop in Nov and Dec. Family targeting needs a children's privacy review, so no under-13 audiences and a legal check on how the parent segment is built.
7. **Rocket Racing sunset.** Rocket Racing ends this month. Moving that audience to other Epic modes is a simple retention play to float.

Open questions for UM: who owns Epic programmatic, whether Epic buys through Omnicom Programmatic deal IDs, and what the current KPI is (installs, reactivation or reach).

## Risks and guardrails

- Children's privacy is the biggest risk on both accounts. No under-13 audiences, parent-household audiences only, and legal review before any gaming-youth segment goes live.
- Chapter 8 dates and the MW4 details can shift. Re-check on the day anything is sent.
- Do not carry Activision numbers into an Epic conversation or the reverse. They compete.
